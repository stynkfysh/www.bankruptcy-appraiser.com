Independent Appraisals for Trustees and Creditors' Counsel

When the value on a debtor's schedules needs a second look, an independent appraisal gives the estate a documented basis for its next step.

Key points

  • Independent, standards-based opinions of value as of the petition date or any other date requested.
  • Desktop and Drive-By reports for screening; inspected Standard reports when access is available.
  • Qualifications and written fee terms are available for court employment applications.
  • Also serves creditors' counsel on motions to value and cramdown disputes.
  • Testimony is available and billed separately.
  • This page is general information from an appraiser, not legal advice. Brian Ward is not a lawyer, and nothing here creates an attorney-client relationship; ask a bankruptcy attorney how the law applies to your case.

Testing the scheduled value

Trustees review hundreds of schedules, and the real property values on them range from careful to optimistic to unexplained. When equity appears to be close to the homestead exemption, or when a scheduled value seems out of step with the neighborhood, an independent appraisal tells the trustee whether pursuing the property makes economic sense for the estate. Brian Ward prepares those reports with the same standards he applies for any client: an opinion of market value as of the effective date requested, supported by closed comparable sales and a plain explanation of adjustments. He does not work toward a target in either direction. A trustee who receives a value lower than hoped has still saved the estate the cost of an unproductive sale effort.

Retrospective values without the guesswork

Most trustee assignments call for value as of the petition date, which may be months before the request reaches us. Brian selects comparable sales that closed before and around that date, analyzes the market trend through it, and avoids importing later price movement into the earlier value. Where the debtor reports post-filing repairs or damage, the report addresses how the property stood on the effective date and states the assumptions it relies on. Older effective dates, such as those involving pre-petition transfers, require more research and are quoted above the base fee. Every report identifies the effective date, the inspection date if any, the intended use, and the intended users, so there is no ambiguity about what the opinion covers.

Access, occupancy, and report type

Trustee assignments vary in how much access is available. When the debtor's counsel arranges an interior inspection, a Standard report at $699 captures condition with photographs and measured data. When access is not practical or the question is preliminary, a Drive-By at $649 confirms exterior condition and setting, and Desktop reports at $449 or $299 can provide a supported estimate for screening purposes. For properties with two to four units, the Desktop costs $549 and the Standard costs $799. Brian will tell you plainly what each level of report can and cannot support, and he notes in the report any limits caused by restricted access, so the estate can judge how much weight to give the result. That disclosure keeps the opinion honest.

Employment and fee terms

When an estate retains a professional, court approval of the employment is generally required, and trustee's counsel typically prepares that application. Brian can provide his qualifications, license information, and written fee terms for use with it. The fee is quoted in writing before work begins. If you need a quick preliminary value before deciding whether to seek employment approval, say so when you call so the assignment can be scoped appropriately. Court appearances, depositions and testimony carry separate charges from the appraisal itself, and those terms are laid out ahead of time. Questions about how fees are paid from the estate are matters for the trustee and the court; Brian will work within whatever arrangement is approved. Invoicing can be arranged to match the estate's process.

Creditors' counsel and secured lenders

Counsel for a secured creditor facing a Chapter 13 motion to value, or a cramdown proposal on a rental or other non-residence property, often needs its own value to test the debtor's figure. Brian prepares those reports independently and on the same footing as his work for debtors: comparable sales chosen on merit, adjustments explained, condition documented as fully as access allows. He can also review a debtor's appraisal and identify where the analysis is thin or where better comparables exist, while keeping in mind that an honest review sometimes confirms the other report. If a matter goes to hearing, he is available to testify about his own opinion of value. Testimony time is billed separately and agreed in advance.
