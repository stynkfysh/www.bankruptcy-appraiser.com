Bankruptcy guide

Appraisal Support for Debtor Attorneys and Their Staff

A straightforward process for law offices that need defensible values on Schedule A/B, plan confirmation, and motions to value.

Key points

  • A state-certified residential appraiser with over 20 years in the field prepares each report himself.
  • Written quote before work; fee due on delivery; firms can be invoiced.
  • Choose Desktop for routine schedules and inspected reports for close equity, condition disputes, and motions.
  • Provide the exact effective date and intended use with each order.
  • Opposing-report review and testimony are available; testimony is billed separately.
  • This page is general information from an appraiser, not legal advice. Brian Ward is not a lawyer, and nothing here creates an attorney-client relationship; ask a bankruptcy attorney how the law applies to your case.

Built around your filing calendar

Most debtor practices work under deadlines that do not move: a foreclosure sale date, a planned filing date, a 341 meeting, an objection deadline, or a confirmation hearing. Brian Ward, a state-certified residential appraiser in California, has done upward of 7,000 assignments since 2004 and prepares each report himself. Orders can be placed by phone at (760) 557-2147 or by email at contact@bankruptcy-appraiser.com. Tell us the address, effective date, chapter, and purpose, and a written quote comes back before work starts. Rush turnaround is often possible when a date is close. Reports can be delivered directly to the office, with a copy to the client if you prefer. Fees are due on delivery, and firms can be invoiced.

Matching the report to the purpose

Different assignments call for different levels of support. When a Chapter 7 home has clear equity below the exemption, a $449 Desktop report or a $299 Basic Desktop frequently supplies a supported Schedule A/B number while keeping the client's cost low. When equity is near the exemption, condition is a central issue, or a trustee has questioned value, an inspected Standard report at $699 provides interior photos and measured data. A Drive-By at $649 confirms exterior and setting. For 2 to 4 unit properties, the Desktop is $549 and the Standard is $799. Lien-strip and cramdown motions usually warrant an inspected report. Complex, luxury, acreage, and older retrospective assignments are quoted above these starting fees. The quote states the fee before work starts.

Effective date and intended use

Each report lists the intended use and users, the effective date, and the inspection date when there was one. Please give us the exact petition date or other effective date you need, and say whether the report is for schedules, a liquidation analysis, a motion to value, or another purpose. If you expect to need values at two dates, such as petition and confirmation, mention it at the outset so the work can be planned together. Brian does not choose the legally relevant date; that is counsel's decision. He does flag factual issues that may bear on it, such as post-filing repairs, damage, or occupancy changes, so you are not surprised later. Assumptions are listed plainly in the report. Clear scoping up front saves revisions later.

Defensible, not advocacy

An appraisal that leans toward the client's preferred number tends to collapse when a trustee or lender reviews it. Brian's reports are independent opinions of value developed under professional appraisal standards. That independence is what makes them useful to you: the comparable sales, adjustments, and condition evidence are laid out so you can explain the value to a trustee and, if necessary, to the court. If the result is not what you hoped for, it is better to learn that before a motion is filed than at an evidentiary hearing. Brian is glad to walk staff through the reasoning in any report, and he will tell you candidly if a different report type would better serve a contested matter. That candor protects your client.

When a case is contested

If a trustee or secured creditor offers a competing value, Brian can review the opposing appraisal or broker opinion with you and identify where the two diverge, whether in comparable selection, adjustments, time trends, or condition. If the matter proceeds to an evidentiary hearing or deposition, he is available to testify about his report. Preparation, depositions and testimony are charged apart from the appraisal, with terms settled before anything is scheduled. Cases from San Diego County are heard in the Southern District of California, and Riverside County cases go to the Central District's Riverside Division. Brian has worked in both counties for two decades and is familiar with the housing markets that drive these disputes. He can also suggest additional comparable sales or analysis if a supplemental report would help.

A note on the limits of this page

This page describes how an appraiser supports bankruptcy practice and is not legal advice. Statements here about exemptions, lien stripping, cramdown, and valuation dates are simplified summaries offered for orientation only; counsel should rely on the Bankruptcy Code, the applicable local rules, and current case law. Brian Ward is not an attorney and does not advise on case strategy, exemption elections, or plan terms. Nothing in a report or conversation should be taken as a prediction of how a trustee or court will rule. What he provides is a credible, well-documented opinion of value as of the date you specify, delivered on the schedule you need. Please contact us with any questions about the scope of a particular assignment. Thank you for considering him.
