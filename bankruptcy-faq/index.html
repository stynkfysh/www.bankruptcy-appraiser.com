FAQ

Bankruptcy appraisal questions, answered

For filers, debtor attorneys and trustees in San Diego and Riverside Counties.

Questions and answers

Do I need an appraisal to file bankruptcy?

The law does not require a formal appraisal for every filing, but you must list your home's value on Schedule A/B under penalty of perjury. Many bankruptcy attorneys ask clients who own real estate to get an appraisal because a professional report is far easier to support than a guess. It matters most when equity is close to the homestead exemption or when a Chapter 13 plan depends on value. Ask your attorney if getting one makes sense for you.

What date should the appraisal value my home as of?

Usually the petition date, which is the day the bankruptcy case was filed, but some Chapter 13 issues may use a different date such as confirmation. Your attorney decides which date the law calls for. Brian then writes the report to that effective date and states it clearly. If the case was filed in the past, the report is retrospective, using sales and market data from around that earlier date.

Can Brian inspect a home outside San Diego or Riverside County?

No. Inspected Standard and Drive-By reports are offered only in the 68 San Diego County and Riverside County communities on the service list. For homes elsewhere in California, Brian prepares desktop reports only: Basic Desktop from $299, Desktop from $449 and Desktop 2-4 Unit from $549. If a case needs an interior inspection outside that area, a local appraiser is the better choice.

Is an online home value estimate good enough?

Automated estimates cannot see inside your home, do not know about damage or deferred maintenance, and are not signed by anyone accountable for them. A trustee may not accept one, and a figure that is too high or too low can each cause problems. An appraisal compares your property with actual closed sales, adjusts for differences, and documents the reasoning, which gives your attorney a value that can be explained and defended.

How much does a bankruptcy appraisal cost?

The floor is $299 (Basic Desktop). A broader Desktop runs from $449, an exterior-only Drive-By from $649, and a fully inspected Standard report on a house or condo from $699. For 2 to 4 unit properties, a Desktop is $549 and a Standard is $799. Complex, luxury, acreage, and older retrospective assignments are quoted higher. You receive a written quote before any work starts, and the fee is due when the report is delivered.

Which type of report should I order?

It depends on how closely the value will be examined. Desktop reports suit typical homes with clear equity and little chance of dispute. Inspected Standard reports are better when equity is near the exemption, when condition problems affect value, or when a lien-stripping motion is planned. Your attorney often knows how the trustee is likely to view the case, so Brian frequently confirms the choice with the law office before scheduling.

Can the appraisal be sent directly to my attorney?

Yes. When you order, include your attorney's name and email, and the report can be delivered to the law office, with a copy to you if you like. Many filers prefer this because the attorney's staff will attach or reference the value in the schedules and plan documents. Law firms that order on behalf of clients can also be invoiced directly.

Will the appraiser try to get a low value for me?

No. Brian provides an independent opinion of market value developed under professional appraisal standards. A report written to reach a target number is easy for a trustee or lender to pick apart and can hurt your case. What he will do is document every legitimate factor that affects value, including condition problems, so nothing that lowers value is overlooked. The result is a figure your attorney can rely on.

What if my house needs major repairs?

Tell Brian about the problems when you order, and if possible choose an inspected report. He photographs damage and deferred maintenance, describes it in the report, and explains how it affects the comparison with homes that sold in better condition. Repair estimates or contractor bids you already have can be helpful supporting information. Condition is one of the most common reasons an appraised value differs from an online estimate.

How long does a bankruptcy appraisal take?

Turnaround depends on the report type, the property, and current scheduling. Desktop reports generally move faster because no visit is needed. Inspected reports require an appointment, and retrospective assignments may need additional research. Rush service is often possible when a filing date, hearing, or 341 meeting is approaching. Mention your deadline when you call (760) 557-2147, and the quote will reflect the timing you need.

What happens at the 341 meeting regarding my home?

The 341 meeting, or meeting of creditors, is where the trustee asks you questions under oath, often by video. Home value is a common topic. If your attorney has already provided an appraisal, you can rely on it rather than explaining how you arrived at a number. The trustee may accept it, ask questions, or seek another opinion. Your attorney handles the legal side of that conversation.

What if the trustee gets a different value?

Differences happen, especially when another opinion was prepared without an interior inspection. Your attorney can compare the two reports, and Brian can explain where and why they differ, such as comparable sales chosen, condition, or timing. Many disagreements are resolved through discussion. If a dispute goes to a hearing, Brian can testify about his report, with testimony billed separately from the appraisal fee.

Can my second mortgage be removed in bankruptcy?

In a Chapter 13 case, a second mortgage or HELOC on your home may be treated as unsecured if the home's value is less than the first mortgage balance, leaving nothing securing the junior loan. This is not available in Chapter 7. It requires a court motion filed by your attorney, and the lien is generally removed only after you complete the plan. An appraisal is the main evidence of value.

Does the homestead exemption protect all of my equity?

Not necessarily. Under California's 704 system, the homestead is the greater of $300,000 or the county median sale price, capped at $600,000, with annual inflation adjustments. Equity above the protected amount may be exposed. California also offers a separate 703 system with a smaller homestead. Which system to use, and the exact amount for your county and filing date, are questions for your bankruptcy attorney.

I own a rental property. Is it valued differently?

Yes, in two ways. Legally, rentals generally do not receive homestead protection, and in Chapter 13 a loan on investment property may be subject to cramdown based on value. For the appraisal itself, buyers of rentals consider rents, unit mix, and condition, so the analysis reflects those factors. Desktop 2 to 4 unit reports start at $549, and inspected Standard 2 to 4 unit reports at $799.

My spouse is not filing. Does that affect the appraisal?

The appraisal values the property itself, regardless of who files. How much of that value belongs to the bankruptcy estate is a legal question, especially in California, where community property rules can bring a non-filing spouse's interest into the picture. Your attorney will sort that out. When you order, mention how title is held and who is filing so the report correctly identifies the property and its owners.

Do you need to come inside my home?

Only for inspected reports. Desktop and Basic Desktop reports are completed without a visit, using public records, listing history, photos, and market data. A Drive-By involves viewing the exterior from the street. A Standard report includes an interior and exterior inspection, typically scheduled at your convenience. If condition issues may lower value, an interior inspection is usually worth it, because photographs are the best evidence of what a buyer would see.

How do I pay, and when is payment due?

You receive a written quote before work starts, and the fee is due when the report is delivered. Payment can be made by card, check, or bank transfer. Law firms ordering for clients can be invoiced. If testimony or a deposition is needed later, that time is billed separately and discussed in advance. Questions about how appraisal costs fit into your bankruptcy budget should go to your attorney.

Is Brian Ward a lawyer? Can he give me legal advice?

No. Brian Ward holds a California residential appraiser certification but is not a lawyer. He can explain how an appraisal works and what it shows, but he cannot advise you on whether to file, which chapter or exemption system to choose, or how a trustee or court will rule. For those decisions, please consult a bankruptcy attorney. Information on this website is general and is not legal advice.

Answers describe appraisal practice, not legal strategy. Ask your bankruptcy attorney how a value will be used in your case.
