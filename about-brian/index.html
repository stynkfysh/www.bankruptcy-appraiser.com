About

Brian Ward, the appraiser behind every report

California Certified Residential Real Estate Appraiser since 2004.

Brian Ward, a state-certified residential appraiser in California, has been valuing homes since 2004. Over more than twenty years he has completed over 7,000 appraisals across San Diego and Riverside Counties, and he still prepares every report himself. That includes the research, the inspection, the analysis, and the signature. Bankruptcy assignments are a regular part of his work, alongside estate, divorce, tax appeal, and litigation appraisals that share the same demand for clear, defensible reasoning. This website belongs to Brian's practice at brianward.com, and his office sits at 15877 Paseo Del Sur, San Diego. When you call (760) 557-2147, Brian is the person you will speak with about your property, your deadline, and the report that fits your case.

Why bankruptcy work suits him

Bankruptcy appraisals are read closely. A trustee compares them with nearby sales, a lender's appraiser may look for weak points, and sometimes a judge has to decide between two opinions. Brian has spent years writing reports for audiences like these, including court-related assignments where every adjustment must be explained. He is comfortable with retrospective effective dates, which many bankruptcy cases require, and with documenting condition problems in a way that holds up. He does not tailor a value to a desired outcome; he believes the most useful thing he can give a filer or an attorney is an honest number they can stand behind when it is questioned.

What he will and will not do

Brian will explain what a report shows, how the value was reached, and which report type fits the purpose. He will tell you if he thinks a different level of report would serve your case better, and he will quote the fee in writing before starting. He will not offer legal advice. Choosing a chapter, an exemption system, or a strategy for a motion belongs to your bankruptcy attorney, and Brian encourages every filer to work with one. He also makes no promises about how a trustee or court will treat a value, because no appraiser can control that. What he can control is the quality and honesty of the work.

Two counties, many kinds of property

Working in both San Diego and Riverside Counties for two decades has given Brian experience with a wide mix of homes: coastal condos with large HOAs, older urban bungalows, newer master-planned subdivisions, horse property and rural acreage, manufactured homes, and desert golf communities. That breadth matters in bankruptcy, where a filer's property may be anything from a starter condo to a fourplex. Cases from San Diego County go to the Southern District of California, and Riverside County cases go to the Central District's Riverside Division. For filers elsewhere in California, desktop bankruptcy appraisals may also be available, subject to data access and market familiarity.
