Homes in Concord

Concord is the largest city in Contra Costa County, and its housing is mainly from the decades after World War II. The Todos Santos area downtown has some older homes and small multifamily buildings. Much of the city, including Holbrook Heights and Sun Terrace, is made up of one-story 1950s and 1960s ranch houses on roomy lots. The Ygnacio Valley and Clayton Valley areas add later subdivisions, some along the foothills of Mount Diablo, with split-level and two-story designs. Condominium and townhouse complexes are common along major roads and near the BART stations. Older homes may have original electrical, plumbing, or windows, while neighbors have fully modernized. For bankruptcy purposes, that gap between original and updated condition is often the biggest driver of value.

How a desktop petition-date value works here

When Brian values a Concord home by desktop, he pairs public data with the details only the owner can supply. The assessor record and MLS history set the baseline: size, age, lot, and how the home looked at its last sale. Permit records show re-roofs, panel upgrades, window replacements, and additions, which help separate an original 1950s ranch from a remodeled one. Aerial and street imagery confirm pools, sheds, and lot shape, and show whether the property backs to a busy road or open space. Because Brian does not inspect, current photos and an honest note about problems from the owner or attorney are essential. His California certified residential license covers Contra Costa County and the rest of the state. The report notes that it is a desktop with no inspection and gives the effective date, commonly the petition date. Concord is a desktop-only city; Standard and Drive-By reports are not offered.

Concord desktop appraisal questions

Can I order a Concord desktop before deciding whether to file?

Yes. Many people get a value first so they and their attorney can review equity and exemptions before filing. If you later file, your attorney may want the value tied to the petition date, which could mean an updated report. Ask for a quote that fits your timing.

What photos should I send for my Concord condo?

Send clear pictures of the living area, kitchen, each bathroom, bedrooms, and any balcony or patio, plus the building exterior if you can. Include photos of any damage, such as water stains or broken fixtures. A copy of your HOA statement showing dues and any special assessment also helps.
