Family Transfer Appraisal

A documented value when a home passes between parents, children, siblings or other relatives.

Moving a home from one family member to another sounds simple until the paperwork starts. A parent may gift a house to a child, siblings may sell shares to each other, or a relative may buy below market to help someone get settled. Each arrangement can raise gift tax questions, property tax reassessment issues, and fairness concerns among other relatives. Brian Ward prepares family transfer appraisals that give everyone a common reference point. He describes the property honestly, chooses sales a typical buyer would consider, and explains the value in plain language. Your CPA and attorney receive a signed report they can attach to returns, deeds or agreements as needed.

Where this meets a bankruptcy case

Family transfers deserve extra care when anyone involved has financial trouble. A bankruptcy trustee has the power to look back at transfers a debtor made before filing, and a home sold to a relative for less than it was worth, or given away outright, can be challenged as a fraudulent transfer. California law and federal law both have look-back periods, and the trustee can seek to recover the property or its value. A contemporaneous appraisal showing that a sale price matched market value at the time can be important evidence that the transaction was fair. If a transfer already happened and a case is now open, a retrospective appraisal as of the transfer date may help the debtor's attorney or the trustee evaluate whether there is a claim. Brian supplies the value only. Anyone considering a transfer while facing debt problems should talk with a bankruptcy attorney before signing anything.

Gifts, sales and partial interests

Family transfers come in many forms. A full gift of a house, a sale at a discounted price, adding a child to title, or selling one sibling's half interest to another all raise the same core question: what is the property worth? For gifts, the value is reported on a federal gift tax return when it exceeds the annual exclusion. For discounted sales, the gap between price and market value may be treated as a gift. Brian's appraisal provides market value for the whole property. If a CPA needs a value for a fractional interest, that requires a different analysis, and Brian will tell you so before you order rather than stretch his report beyond its purpose.

Property tax and reassessment questions

California rules on parent-child and grandparent-grandchild transfers changed in recent years, and whether a transfer triggers reassessment can depend on how the home is used afterward and on its value at the time. County assessors in San Diego and Riverside each process these claims, and the forms can ask for market value information. Brian does not interpret tax law or file claims, but a current appraisal gives your advisor a solid number to work with when calculating what might be excluded and what might be reassessed. Ask your CPA or estate attorney which date and value they need before ordering, so the report fits the forms. Keep a copy of the appraisal with the deed and the claim forms for future reference.

Keeping the family on the same page

Transfers within families can create quiet resentment if one relative suspects another received a better deal. A neutral, written appraisal helps prevent that. Everyone can see the comparable sales and the reasoning, and the transaction no longer depends on a family member's opinion or an online estimate. Brian is happy to speak with more than one relative to answer questions about how he reached the number. When a parent wants to treat children equally, one child receiving a home and another receiving cash or other assets, the appraisal becomes the yardstick for that balance. The report can name multiple family members as intended users. That record can matter years later, when memories have faded and someone asks how the figure was set.

What the report includes

  • Opinion of market value as of the transfer date or a current date
  • Comparable sales grid with plain-English explanations
  • Photographs and description of condition (inspected reports)
  • Retrospective value for a past transfer when requested
  • Location map, scope of work and signed certification
  • Report naming family members, CPA or attorney as intended users

Family Transfer Appraisal questions

Do I need an appraisal to gift my house to my child?

A gift above the annual exclusion generally must be reported, and the IRS expects a supportable value. A qualified appraisal is the usual way to provide one. Property tax forms may also call for value information. Your CPA or estate attorney can confirm what your situation requires. Keep the appraisal with the gift return records.

Can a family member sell to me below market value?

They can, but the discount may be treated as a gift for tax purposes, and if the seller later files bankruptcy, a trustee may examine the sale. An appraisal documents the true market value so everyone understands the size of the discount. Get legal and tax advice before closing. A written value protects everyone involved.

Is this the same appraisal a lender would order?

No. If you are financing the purchase, the lender will order its own appraisal through its approved channels. A family transfer appraisal is ordered by you or your advisors for tax, legal and fairness purposes. The two reports may reach similar values, but they serve different users. Neither can stand in for the other.

Can you value a transfer that happened years ago?

Yes. A retrospective appraisal uses sales from the period around the transfer date. This is common when a CPA catches an unreported gift or when a trustee reviews a past transaction. The quote reflects how far back the date reaches and how much research is involved. Older dates require more digging in the records.
