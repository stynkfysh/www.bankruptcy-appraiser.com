Property Tax Appeal Appraisal

Evidence for a decline-in-value review or assessment appeal, built around the January 1 lien date.

California property taxes are tied to assessed value, which normally rises a little each year. When market value falls below that assessed figure, owners may qualify for a temporary reduction under Proposition 8, and some owners also challenge a new base year value after a purchase or construction. Both paths need evidence. Brian Ward prepares appraisals focused on the lien date the assessor uses, typically January 1, with comparable sales close to that date. The report is written for county assessors and assessment appeals boards in San Diego County and Riverside County. Brian does not file appeals or represent owners at hearings, but he supplies the valuation evidence your filing needs.

Where this meets a bankruptcy case

Property taxes and bankruptcy connect in a few practical ways. Unpaid property taxes are secured by the home, and in a Chapter 13 case they may be paid through the plan, so a lower assessment can reduce future obligations and ease plan feasibility. A debtor whose home value has dropped may find that the same evidence supporting a Proposition 8 reduction also supports the value listed on Schedule A/B, although the effective dates often differ. Trustees reviewing a property's equity sometimes look at assessed value, and a well-supported appraisal explains why market value may be lower or higher than the tax roll suggests. Brian can coordinate reports for the lien date and the petition date when both are needed, with clear notes on why the values differ. Whether pursuing a tax appeal makes sense during a bankruptcy is a question for the debtor's attorney.

Proposition 8 decline-in-value reviews

Proposition 8 allows a temporary reduction when a property's market value on January 1 is lower than its factored base year value. Each county runs its own informal review and formal appeal process, with filing windows that change from year to year. An informal review request may be enough when the evidence is clear. Should the assessor turn down the request, the owner can take the matter to the assessment appeals board through a formal appeal. In either case, an appraisal using sales that closed near the lien date gives the assessor or board something concrete to consider. Brian focuses on sales that a reviewer would recognize as truly comparable. The report states the lien date on its first page so there is no confusion about timing.

Base year value challenges

When a home is purchased or newly built, the county sets a new base year value, usually based on the purchase price or construction cost. If that value seems too high, perhaps because the price included personal property, seller concessions or unusual terms, an owner may appeal. The appraisal then focuses on market value as of the change in ownership or completion date. Brian reviews the transaction details you provide and develops a value based on comparable sales from that period. The report explains any differences between the price paid and the market value, which helps the board understand the claim. Documents such as the closing statement and any credits from the seller are especially helpful here.

Deadlines and preparation

Appeal deadlines are strict, and missing one usually means waiting another year. Check the county assessor's website or your tax bill for current filing periods, and order the appraisal early enough to meet them. Gather your purchase documents, any recent repair records, and photos showing condition near the lien date. If the home has issues that a drive-by would miss, such as foundation cracks or an outdated interior, an inspected report usually presents them more persuasively. Brian provides a written quote and delivery date up front so you can plan around the filing window without last-minute surprises. Late requests are sometimes possible, but tight timing can limit which report types are available.

Property Tax Appeal Appraisal questions

Do I need an appraisal to request a Proposition 8 review?

Not always. Some counties accept owner-provided sales information for informal reviews. An appraisal becomes more important when the assessor denies the request, when the case goes to a formal appeal, or when the property is hard to compare. It gives your claim professional support. It also helps when the decline is modest and needs careful support.

Which date does a tax appeal appraisal use?

The valuation date for a Proposition 8 review is usually January 1 of the tax year being reviewed. Base year challenges use the date of purchase or completion of construction. Confirm the date with the assessor or your tax advisor before ordering so the report matches the claim. A report with the wrong date may not be useful to the board.

Will you represent me at the appeals board hearing?

Brian does not act as a tax agent or file appeals on your behalf. He can appear to explain his appraisal if needed, with testimony billed separately. Many owners present their own cases using the report, while others work with a tax consultant or attorney. The report is written to be understood by board members.

Can a lower assessment help my Chapter 13 plan?

Lower property taxes can reduce housing costs, which may affect your budget during the plan. Whether pursuing an appeal makes sense during your case is something to discuss with your bankruptcy attorney. Brian can provide values for the lien date and petition date if both are needed. The dates usually differ.
