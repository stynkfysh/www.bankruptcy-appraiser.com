Immigration I-864 Appraisal

Documented home equity for sponsors who use real estate as an asset on the Affidavit of Support.

Sponsors who file Form I-864 must show they can support the immigrant they are sponsoring. If income is not enough by itself, assets can fill the gap, and the government counts real estate equity among them. Proving that equity typically takes an appraisal by a certified or licensed appraiser plus documentation of the loan balance. Brian Ward prepares these reports for sponsors and joint sponsors in San Diego County and Riverside County. The appraisal states the property's market value clearly so that your immigration attorney can subtract the loans and present net equity. Brian does not give immigration advice, but he delivers the value document promptly and in a clean format.

Where this meets a bankruptcy case

A sponsor's financial picture matters on the I-864, and bankruptcy can complicate it. If a sponsor has an open bankruptcy case, the home is part of the estate, and any equity may be claimed through the homestead exemption or reached by a trustee. Immigration officers may ask about recent bankruptcies when weighing whether the sponsor's assets are truly available. A sponsor who completed a Chapter 7 discharge some time ago may still own the home and want to use its equity; a current appraisal shows what that equity looks like today. A sponsor in a Chapter 13 plan has ongoing obligations that reduce available income. In each case, the appraisal is one input, and the immigration attorney and bankruptcy attorney should coordinate on how the sponsor's situation is presented. Brian provides an honest market value and nothing more, which keeps the document credible for every reader.

How home equity counts on the I-864

The Affidavit of Support uses an income threshold based on household size. When a sponsor's income does not meet it, assets can fill the gap, generally at a multiple of the shortfall that varies by the type of immigrant. For real estate, the asset value is net equity: market value minus mortgages and other liens. A primary residence may count, though the rules and how officers view it can vary. That is why the appraisal should be paired with a current mortgage statement and guidance from your immigration attorney. Brian's report supplies the market value side of that equation in a form attorneys are used to receiving. Order the appraisal after confirming with counsel that real estate equity will be used.

What the government expects to see

Instructions for the I-864 refer to documentation of asset value, and for real estate that typically means an appraisal by a licensed or certified appraiser rather than a tax bill or online estimate. The report should identify the property, the owner, the effective date, and the value conclusion clearly. Brian's reports include his California certification, a description of the property, comparable sales, and photographs when the home is inspected. Keeping the effective date recent helps, since an outdated value may raise questions. If your case stretches over many months, ask your attorney whether an update will be needed before an interview, and Brian can provide one. Updates are usually quicker than the original report because the property is already familiar.

Joint sponsors and rental properties

Sometimes the equity comes from a joint sponsor or a household member rather than the petitioner. The property may be a rental, a condo, or a small multi-unit building rather than the sponsor's own residence. Brian appraises each type and states who owns the property according to the information provided, so the attorney can connect it to the right person on the forms. Rental properties may also generate income that appears elsewhere on the I-864. When more than one property is used, Brian can deliver reports together with matching effective dates, which keeps the evidence package organized and simple for an officer to review. Ownership details should match the deed, so share a copy if the title is held in more than one name.

Immigration I-864 Appraisal questions

Is an appraisal required to use home equity on the I-864?

The form instructions call for evidence of an asset's value, and for real estate an appraisal by a licensed or certified appraiser is the standard way to show it. Your immigration attorney can confirm what your case needs and whether a primary residence will count in your situation. Some cases rely on other assets instead.

Does the appraisal calculate my equity?

The appraisal provides market value. Equity is that value minus what you owe, which comes from your mortgage statements and any other lien documents. Your attorney typically does the subtraction and presents both documents together. Brian can note known liens if you provide them, but his conclusion is market value.

How recent does the appraisal need to be?

There is no fixed rule, but a recent effective date is more persuasive. If the case runs long or an officer requests updated evidence, a fresh report may be needed. Ask your attorney about timing before ordering so the report is current when it matters. Updates are typically quoted at a reduced fee when nothing about the property has changed.

I filed bankruptcy a few years ago. Can I still use my home?

Possibly. A completed bankruptcy does not automatically prevent you from using home equity, but officers may look at your overall financial history. An immigration attorney should advise on how to present it. Brian can provide a current market value regardless of past filings. Honest disclosure of the past filing is usually the best approach.
